Dilemma 1. Introduce the box in the corner: "we collect four conditions as we go". SAY: "First dilemma: is Ethiopia one state, or a conglomerate of nations? More than eighty peoples. In the last census, 2007, the Oromo were about 35%, the Amhara about 27%, Somali and Tigrayans about 6% each. In 1969 a student, Walleligne Makonnen, wrote that Ethiopia was not one nation but a dozen nationalities: 'the national question'. Every regime since answered it by force: the emperor by assimilation, the military junta by terror, the TPLF by ethnic federalism. Part 1How landlocked states think

Three ways
to think

1 · Vulnerability

The neighbour with the port holds your lifeline. Every closure is a threat.

2 · Adaptation

Many partners, many ports: Djibouti, Berbera, Port Sudan, Assab.

3 · Interdependence

Shared corridors and rules, so that no single neighbour can close the door.

Switzerland, Botswana: rules. Afghanistan, Armenia: isolation.
The framework of the article: Mahdi (2021), "Security and foreign policy of landlocked states", as used by Woldemariam (2026, pp. 2–3). SAY: "How does a state without a sea think? Woldemariam uses a framework by Mahdi, with three structural conditions. One, vulnerability: you depend on a transit neighbour; whoever has the port holds your lifeline, so every closure feels like a threat. Two, adaptation: you diversify, many partners, many ports. Ethiopia has tried Djibouti, Berbera in Somaliland, Port Sudan, Lamu in Kenya, and Assab. Three, interdependence: you build shared corridors and rules, so that no single neighbour can close the door. The comparisons in the article: Switzerland and Botswana made it work through institutions and interdependence. Afghanistan and Armenia show the other path: instability at home and nationalist narratives, which deepen vulnerability and isolation. Keep the question for later: which path is Ethiopia on?"

Part 2 · How we got here1889 – 1896

An empire
by conquest

Menelik II conquers the south, east and west. Today's borders are drawn by force.

Italy takes the coast in 1890 and calls it Eritrea. Ethiopia beats Italy at Adwa, 1896.

Rewind effect. Warm, faded colours mean: we are in the past. SAY: "1889. Menelik II, emperor from the central province of Shewa, conquers the south, the east and the west. The empire doubles. Today's borders are drawn by conquest, and the conquered peoples, Oromo, Somali, the peoples of the south, are ruled from an Amharic-speaking centre. That is the root of dilemma one. At the same time Italy takes the coast and calls it Eritrea: the root of dilemma two. In 1896 Ethiopia crushes Italy at Adwa, which is why it was never a colony. But Eritrea lives fifty years apart, as an Italian colony, with its own identity. (Mussolini occupies Ethiopia in 1936; the British drive the Italians out in 1941.)"

The UN
draws a line

1950: the UN (Res. 390 A) federates Eritrea with Ethiopia. The emperor gets his coast.

Eritreans are never asked in a vote.

1962: the emperor annexes Eritrea. The UN says nothing.

The first empty chair, and the birth of the mistrust.
Course hook: the UN's first decision on this conflict. SAY: "1950: the UN General Assembly, Resolution 390 A, decides that the former Italian colony becomes an autonomous Eritrea federated with Ethiopia, with its own parliament. Emperor Haile Selassie had lobbied for exactly this: it gave his empire a coast. Look at who sits at the table: the UN, the emperor, the great powers; the Americans wanted a listening base in Asmara. Eritreans never voted. The first empty chair. 1962: the emperor dissolves the Eritrean parliament and annexes Eritrea. The UN, which built the arrangement, says nothing. This is where Eritrean mistrust is born: all four conditions are already on the board."

Thirty years
of war

1974: a military junta, the Derg, topples the emperor. Terror, famine, Soviet arms.

Eritrean and Tigrayan guerrillas fight it: the EPLF and the TPLF.

Keep it fast: this is the bridge to 1991. SAY: "Who keeps the annexation alive? Outside patrons. First the Americans, with their base in Asmara and arms for the emperor. In 1974 a military junta, the Derg, overthrows the emperor; from 1977 the Soviet Union arms Mengistu's regime, with Cuban troops. The patron changes, the war does not. Terror, and the famine of 1984. Two guerrilla fronts fight the Derg: the EPLF in Eritrea, the TPLF in Tigray. They are allies, but uneasy ones; they quarrel in the 1980s over strategy and borders. Remember that: the seed of a later mistrust. 1991: they win. Mengistu flees. 1993: Eritreans vote 99.8% for independence, observed by the UN. Ethiopia becomes a country without a coast."
Part 21991 – 2018

Tigray
at the top

The TPLF, from a region of 6%, leads the coalition that rules Ethiopia for 27 years: the EPRDF.

It accepts Eritrea's independence. Ethiopia becomes landlocked under a Tigrayan-led government.

It redraws the country by ethnicity, with a right to secede (Art. 39).

Nationalists still blame the TPLF for losing the sea.
The key fact of Part 2: a minority at the top of a landlocked state. SAY: "1991. The TPLF, a front from a region with about 6% of the population, leads the coalition that takes power in Addis: the EPRDF. Its leader, Meles Zenawi, rules Ethiopia until his death in 2012, and the TPLF dominates the security apparatus until 2018. It accepts Eritrea's independence; the EPLF were its wartime allies. So Ethiopia becomes landlocked under a Tigrayan-led government. Ethiopian nationalists, and in 2023 Abiy himself, called giving up the coast a historic mistake. And it answers the national question by redrawing the country by ethnicity: nine regions, and Article 39, the right of every 'nation, nationality and people' to secede. Empty chair again: in 1992 the Oromo Liberation Front, speaking for the largest group, walks out of the transition. Ethiopia's dilemmas now have a Tigrayan face."
Part 2 · The seminar textAbbink 2011

The federalism
trap

1 · Containers

Each people owns a territory. Every border becomes a prize, like Western Tigray's farmland.

2 · One lock

One party runs every region. Federal on paper, "more like a unitary state" (p. 602).

3 · A right that sleeps

Art. 39 is "vacuous" while the centre is strong (p. 605). The West pays the regime.

4 · If the lock breaks?

Every container has its own government, its own police, and a right to leave.

A federation that works only while one party holds the key.
THE SEMINAR TEXT. Jon Abbink, "Ethnic-based federalism and ethnicity in Ethiopia: reassessing the experiment after 20 years", Journal of Eastern African Studies 5(4), 2011, 596–618. Written nine years before the war. Slow down. 1) Containers: "Ethnicity becomes territory. Every internal border is now a prize: land, budget, jobs, who counts as 'native'. Abbink counts dozens of boundary conflicts, 5,000 to 6,000 dead in twenty years (p. 605). His own example: the TPLF attached the sesame lands of Humera and Wolkait, Western Tigray, to Tigray on 'profitable economic criteria' (p. 608). Amhara nationalists call it 'our historical land'." 2) One lock: "Why didn't it explode? Because one party ran every container. Federal on paper, 'more like a unitary state' (p. 602), 'stronger than any previous Ethiopian state' (p. 596)." 3) A right that sleeps: "Article 39 is 'vacuous': you cannot use a right to leave against a centre that strong. And the West pays: Meles is a donor favourite and Washington's partner against terrorism in Somalia." 4) If the lock breaks? "Abbink's warning: if the party ever loses control, every container has its own government, its own police, and a constitutional right to leave." Punchline: "A federation that works only while one party holds the key. In 2018, the key changes hands."

WhyPorts, trade, a new currency, and two new regimes using the war at home.

Gergő covers this war in detail; we only need what it did to the relationship. One sentence on screen at a time. SAY: "1998: the two winners of 1991, Meles in Addis and Isaias in Asmara, go to war over a dusty border town, Badme. Between 70 and 100 thousand dead. Why? Port fees in Assab, trade, Eritrea's new currency in 1997, and, as Woldemariam argues, two new regimes using the conflict at home: diversionary politics after the fall of the Derg. 2002: after the Algiers peace, an international commission rules, 'final and binding', that Badme is Eritrean. Ethiopia refuses. Badme is administered by Tigray, by the TPLF, and they will not hand it over: an empty chair. Then sixteen years of 'no war, no peace': Eritrea squeezes out the UN mission in 2008, each side arms the other's rebels, and in 2009 the Security Council sanctions Eritrea (Res. 1907). Here the mistrust becomes personal: Isaias against the TPLF. That explains 2018 and 2020."

Part 3: demography meets geopolitics. This is the turning point of the story. SAY: "From 2015, mass protests in Oromia and then Amhara. Together these two peoples are about 60% of Ethiopians, and they felt ruled by a party from a region of 6%. In April 2018 the coalition gives up and picks Abiy Ahmed, an Oromo, as prime minister. He releases prisoners, invites the exiled rebels home, and puts TPLF-era security chiefs on trial for corruption and abuses. The TPLF leaders retreat to Mekelle. In December 2019 Abiy merges the coalition into a single new party, the Prosperity Party. The TPLF refuses to join. Abbink's lock is broken. But here is the dangerous part: Tigray keeps a strong regional special force and militia, and the federal army's Northern Command, a large part of its heavy weapons, is based in Tigray, facing Eritrea. Demoted at the centre, armed at home. A region that feels pushed out, and has the means to resist."

Part 32018 – 2020

A peace
against someone

2018: Abiy makes peace with Eritrea. Nobel Prize, 2019.

A deal between two men, against one common enemy: the TPLF.

Signed in Jeddah, without the AU. No treaty. The ports never open.

2020: Tigray votes alone. Each side calls the other illegal.

SAY: "2018: Abiy accepts the border ruling and makes peace with Eritrea after twenty years. Nobel Peace Prize in 2019. But Isaias answers in the same speech: 'game over' for the TPLF. For Asmara, Woldemariam writes, the new leadership in Addis was the end of TPLF influence: a leadership Eritrea could finally trust. The peace has a target, and the TPLF, which actually holds Badme, has an empty chair. It is personal and Gulf-made: the final signing is in Jeddah before the Saudi king, the Emirates pledge three billion dollars to Addis. Kimaita, in the same HORN issue, notes it was negotiated without the AU or IGAD. No treaty ratified by parliament, no joint commissions; the border crossings reopen and close again within months; Ethiopia's access to Assab and Massawa is never put into practice. 2020: elections are postponed for COVID, Tigray votes anyway; Addis declares the vote void, Mekelle declares Abiy's mandate expired. Each side calls the other illegal. All four conditions are lit."

Part 5Who got what

what?

Addis

The TPLF disarmed, the army back in Mekelle, federal authority restored. Most of what it wanted.

TPLF

Survival, the siege lifted, an interim government, off the terror list (2023). Paid with its heavy weapons.

Postponed

Interests and gains, plainly. SAY: "Who got what? Addis wanted the TPLF disarmed and its authority back, and it got most of it: the federal army returned to Mekelle and the airports, the TPLF handed over its heavy weapons in January 2023 and accepted the constitution. The TPLF wanted to survive, and it did: the siege was lifted, banks, power and phones came back, an interim regional administration was set up in March 2023 with Getachew Reda as president, and parliament removed the TPLF from the terror list. The price: its heavy weapons. The AU got a success for 'African solutions to African problems', without the means to enforce it. And three things were postponed: Western Tigray, the Eritrean troops who never fully left, and accountability for war crimes. Every postponed question has an army behind it."

Three mistrusts. This is the bridge to the war of 2026. SAY: "Pretoria silenced the guns, but it could not fix three mistrusts. Eritrea, outside: Woldemariam writes that Asmara read its exclusion as a threat to its security and its regional standing. It had fought the war to destroy the TPLF; now the TPLF survives, armed, and Asmara has no say in Mekelle. So Eritrea will keep trying to influence both Tigray and Addis from outside. The Amhara forces, outside: they hold Western Tigray, and Pretoria postponed it as 'contested areas'. And Tigray itself, inside but wary: it wants to keep its de facto autonomy and its own force. After two years of siege and famine, it does not trust Addis to protect it. Look at the last arrow: a question mark between Asmara and Mekelle. Hold that thought."

Part 7The pattern

Four conditions,
every time

1 · Many nations
