🗺️ Part 1 · The map · slides 4–11

Three dilemmas, and why Tigray sits on all of them. Watch the box in the corner fill up.

04🌍 The Horn of Africa40 s

🎬 Meet the three characters and the sea.

  • 🇪🇹 👆 Ethiopia: 130 million, second biggest in Africa, no coast since 1993.
  • 🇪🇷 👆 Eritrea: ~3.5 million (no census ever), the whole coast: Assab and Massawa.
  • 🟨 👆 Tigray: northern region, 6 million, wedged between Eritrea and Sudan.
  • 🌊 👆 The Red Sea: ~12% of world trade. "Every time the blue line lights up, the sea is the reason."

05🧩 Dilemma 1 · one state, many nations?55 s

🎬 The first crack in the story: who owns the state?

  • 👥 👆 80+ peoples. Oromo ~35%, Amhara ~27%, Somali and Tigrayans ~6% each (2007 census).
  • 🏠 👆 Since 1991 each region is an ethnic homeland, with its own government and police.
  • ✊ 👆 1969: student Walleligne Makonnen: "not one nation, a dozen nationalities". Every regime answered by force.
  • 📦 Point to the box top-left: "We'll collect four conditions. This is the first."

💬 "Whoever holds the centre holds the state."

06⚓ Dilemma 2 · a giant without a sea45 s

🎬 The second crack: the lost coast.

  • 🏳️ 👆 1993: Eritrea's independence takes the whole coast.
  • 🚢 👆 Over 90% of Ethiopia's trade goes through one port: Djibouti.
  • 🔒 👆 One corridor, high fees, no leverage: the sea turns from trade into security.
  • 🗨️ 👆 Abiy: sea access is "legal, historical and existential".

➡️ "How does a country without a sea think?"

07🧠 Three ways to think50 s

🎬 The lens from the HORN article (Woldemariam, using Mahdi).

  • 😰 👆 Vulnerability: the neighbour with the port holds your lifeline.
  • 🔀 👆 Adaptation: many partners, many ports: Djibouti, Berbera, Port Sudan, Assab.
  • 🤝 👆 Interdependence: shared corridors and rules, so no neighbour can close the door.
  • 🇨🇭 👆 Switzerland and Botswana chose rules; Afghanistan and Armenia, nationalism and isolation.

💬 "Keep this question: which path is Ethiopia on?"

08⚖️ Two logics50 s

🎬 Put the two neighbours side by side.

  • 📊 Row by row 👆: 130 m vs 3.5 m · no sea vs the whole coast.
  • 🏛️ Regime: a federation with bureaucracies vs a one-party state, no national election, Isaias since 1991.
  • 🎯 Logic: Ethiopia wants partners and trade; Eritrea thinks security first, its ports are its only card.

💬 "For Ethiopia the coast is a lifeline. For Eritrea it is leverage."

09🧊 Dilemma 3 · built-in mistrust45 s

🎬 The third crack: two neighbours who read each other wrong.

  • 🧠 👆 Asmara remembers: 1962 annexation, 30 years of war, 1998.
  • 😟 👆 Addis fears: an Eritrea that arms rebels and holds the door to the sea.
  • 🔁 👆 The trap: each side's defence looks like the other's attack.

💬 "Every peace so far was made between leaders, not institutions."

10📍 Why Tigray?50 s

🎬 Zoom in: our region sits on the fault line.

  • 🧱 👆 The frontier: Badme, Zalambessa, Irob, every border war is fought here.
  • 🚪 👆 The back door: its west touches Sudan (farmland, refugees, guns).
  • 🪖 👆 The army: the TPLF ruled Ethiopia 27 years and still has its own force.
  • ☠️ 👆 The enemy: for Asmara an armed TPLF is an "existential threat".

💬 "Tigray sits on all three dilemmas at once."

➡️ "But why 'existential'? Let's look from Asmara."

11🇪🇷 Why "existential" for Asmara?50 s

🎬 Switch the camera to Asmara: why would Isaias fear a regional party next door?

  • 🧠 👆 Memory: the TPLF ran the state that fought Eritrea in 1998, kept Badme, lobbied for UN sanctions (2009).
  • 📏 👆 Next door: an organised army right on the border, ~100 km from Asmara.
  • 👑 👆 The regime: TPLF-era Addis hosted Eritrean opposition groups. No elections in Eritrea, so Isaias's legitimacy rests on defending the nation; a strong hostile Tigray could help topple him.
  • 🗣️ 👆 Same people: Tigrinya speakers on both sides of the border; a strong Tigray competes for the same people and the same past.

💬 "Not a rival party: a rival state next door. That's why he fought it in 2020, and allied with it in 2025: the goal is to control what Tigray does."

➡️ "How did we get here? Rewind." ◀◀

⏪ Part 2 · How we got here · slides 12–17

Sepia colours = the past. Each scene lights a condition in the box. Keep it fast.

12👑 An empire by conquest · 188935 s

  • ⚔️ 👆 Menelik II conquers south, east, west: borders drawn by force, peoples ruled from an Amharic centre. Root of dilemma 1.
  • 🇮🇹 👆 Italy takes the coast, calls it Eritrea. Root of dilemma 2. Adwa 1896: Ethiopia beats Italy, never a colony.

➡️ "After World War II, the UN has to decide what to do with Eritrea."

13🇺🇳 The UN draws a line · 195045 s

  • 📜 👆 Res. 390 A: Eritrea federated with Ethiopia; the emperor gets his coast.
  • 🪑 👆 The table: UN, emperor, US/UK… Eritreans never voted. The first empty chair.
  • 🔨 👆 1962: annexation. The UN says nothing.

💬 "The first empty chair, and the birth of the mistrust."

14🔥 Thirty years of war · 1961–199335 s

  • ⭐ 👆 1974: the Derg junta topples the emperor. Terror, famine, Soviet arms.
  • 🏔️ 👆 Two guerrilla fronts fight it: EPLF (Eritrea) and TPLF (Tigray), uneasy allies.
  • 🏳️ 👆 1991 they win; 1993 Eritrea is independent.

💬 "Ethiopia loses the sea."

15🔝 Tigray at the top · 199150 s

🎬 The twist: a 6% region now runs the whole country.

  • 🏛️ 👆 The TPLF leads the EPRDF coalition, rules 27 years (Meles Zenawi).
  • ⚓ 👆 It accepts Eritrea's independence: Ethiopia becomes landlocked under a Tigrayan-led government.
  • 🗺️ 👆 It redraws the map by ethnicity, with a right to secede (Art. 39). The Oromo front walks out.

💬 "Nationalists still blame the TPLF for losing the sea."

16📖 Abbink · the federalism trap70 s

🎬 Your seminar text. Slow down: this is the heart of the history.

  • 📦 👆 Containers: each people owns land; every border becomes a prize (Western Tigray, p. 608).
  • 🔐 👆 One lock: one party runs everything, "more like a unitary state" (p. 602).
  • 😴 👆 A right that sleeps: Art. 39 is "vacuous" while the centre is strong (p. 605); the West pays.
  • 💣 👆 If the lock breaks: every container has a government, a police, a right to leave.

💬 "A federation that works only while one party holds the key."

17🥊 Brothers at war · 1998–201850 s

🎬 The two winners of 1991 turn on each other. (Gergő covers details; keep it short.)

  • 💥 👆 1998–2000: Badme war, 70–100k dead.
  • ❓ 👆 Why: port fees, trade, a new currency, regimes using war at home.
  • ⚖️ 👆 2002: "final and binding" ruling gives Badme to Eritrea; Ethiopia and Tigray refuse.
  • 🧊 👆 UN mission squeezed out, proxy rebels: no war, no peace.

💬 "The mistrust becomes personal: Isaias against the TPLF."

➡️ "And then, in 2018, the key changes hands."

  • 🇸🇩 👆 Sudan: army vs RSF; Egypt/Saudi vs UAE-linked supplies (denied). Tigray's back door.
